# Scenario E07: the Family Assistant home

Generated by `cargo run --release -p idbd-lab --bin family-eval`. idbd-lab 0.1.0, generator xoshiro256** seeded by SplitMix64, Box-Muller normals (v1).

## Method

The home in `idbd_lab::family` has 12 sensors. Each reading is an independent standard normal times the sensor's own reading scale. The target is the power draw above the always-on base, in kW: the sum, over the sensors active at that hour, of the influence times the standardized reading, plus Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.5. The noise alone gives a mean squared error of 0.25.

| Sensor | Reading scale | Adds to the power draw | kW per standard deviation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wi-Fi signal | 1 | never | 0 |
| Water heater temperature | 0.5 | 06:00 to 08:00 | 1.2 |
| Doorbell battery | 2 | never | 0 |
| Wall temperature | 1 | 05:00 to 09:00 | 0.9 |
| Barometer | 0.5 | never | 0 |
| Oven temperature | 2 | 17:00 to 20:00 | 1 |
| Humidity | 1 | never | 0 |
| CO2 | 2 | never | 0 |
| Wi-Fi data use | 0.5 | 20:00 to 23:00 | 0.6 |
| Air quality (PM2.5) | 1 | never | 0 |
| Front door | 2 | never | 0 |
| Plant soil moisture | 0.5 | never | 0 |

One reading arrives per simulated minute, 60 an hour, for 3 days. The error is averaged from the start of the second day. Every learner starts from zero weights, and IDBD starts every step size at 0.05.

A learner's bar for a sensor and an hour is its weight, scaled to kW per standard deviation of the reading and averaged over the readings of that hour on all 3 days. The bar marks an hour as found when it reaches 0.5 of the sensor's peak, unless the peak stays below 0.25 kW, in which case the sensor is found to never matter. A sensor's score is the overlap of the found hours with the true hours: shared hours over hours in either. The bar score pools that over all 12 sensors: hours marked right on any sensor over hours marked by the bars or the truth. Leaving every sensor empty scores 0 and every wrongly marked hour lowers the score.

The LMS step size and the IDBD meta step size are each selected by mean squared error on tuning seeds 1001, 1002, 1003, 1004, 1005, then reported on evaluation seeds 1 to 10. A grid value with any failed run is not selectable. Learning speeds are reported per sensor as `alpha_i * scale_i^2`, the step size in units of a standardized reading.

## What these numbers do and do not say

The sensors read on different scales, and one fixed step size cannot suit all of them: it is slow on the sensors with small readings and noisy on those with large ones. IDBD gives every sensor its own step size. Rescaling every reading to a standard deviation of 1 before learning, which the `idbd` README recommends, removes most of that difference, and a tuned fixed step size then draws bars close to IDBD's.

Bars trail the true hours. A weight needs time to grow after its sensor starts to matter and to fade after it stops, so a found range can start or end an hour late, most often on sensors with small readings.

Step sizes do not mark hours on this task: a sensor that matters at dinner every day keeps a high step size around the clock. The bars therefore come from the weights, and the step sizes are reported per sensor.

The home is synthetic. The learners predict the power draw from the readings and switch nothing on or off.
